El Niño, the monsoon and India's rice economy

A weak late-season monsoon can travel from Pacific Ocean temperatures to farm yields, food prices and export markets. Today's brief connects the science with what it means for India.

6 min read · Updated 18 Sep, 6:30 PM IST · 8 checked sources

What changed today

Monsoon withdrawal is near

IMD says conditions are becoming favourable for withdrawal from parts of West Rajasthan around 19 September. A low-pressure area may also form over the Andaman Sea.

How the chain works

Pacific waters warm and trade winds weaken.
Global rainfall patterns shift during El Niño.
Less rain during rice maturation can reduce yields.
Tighter supply can lift local and export prices.

Why this matters

Rice is more than a crop. It links household food budgets, farm income, public stocks and India's role in global trade.

ISRO's EOS-06 satellite has observed lower surface chlorophyll and changing winds in the equatorial Pacific, signs consistent with a developing El Niño. Those ocean changes help scientists track a climate pattern that can influence India's monsoon.

The immediate outlook is not one simple national story. IMD's 18 September bulletin reports heavy rain risks in several regions even as the seasonal withdrawal approaches in the northwest. Local conditions matter crop by crop and district by district.
